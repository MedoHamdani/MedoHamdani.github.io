Tawzan: A Hamdani Village in Bani Makram, Sana’a

A short historical and demographic report on Tawzan village in Hamdan district, its tribal affiliation, and local context.

Yemen & Heritage • Reading time: 6 min

Overview

Tawzan is a village in Yemen located in Hamdan District, Sana’a Governorate, within the subdistrict of Bani Makram. It is part of the broader tribal and geographic world of Hamdan, one of the historically significant tribal territories in the Yemeni highlands.

Demographics

  • 2004 census: 2,012 people
  • 2014 estimate: 2,703 people

Tribal and Geographic Context

Hamdan is a historically recognized tribal region in Yemen. The village of Tawzan belongs to Bani Makram, which is one of the known localities within Hamdan. This area is part of a wider pattern of highland Yemeni settlement where tribal identity, local geography, and agricultural organization are closely linked.

Yemeni highland villages reflect centuries of agricultural and tribal settlement patterns.

Local Life

Villages like Tawzan are typical examples of community-based rural life in the Sana’a basin. They participate in the social fabric of Hamdan, maintain local tribal customs, and are tied to regional agricultural and water-management traditions.

Significance

Even though Tawzan is small in population, it is part of Yemen’s larger cultural landscape. Documenting villages like Tawzan helps preserve knowledge about Yemeni rural geography and local heritage.
