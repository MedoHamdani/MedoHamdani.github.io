Flat Feet & Knee Pain: The Hidden Connection

Understanding flat feet, how they affect your knees, and evidence-based exercises to strengthen your feet and reduce pain.

Normal arch vs flat arch: notice the collapsed medial longitudinal arch in pes planus.

What Are Flat Feet?

Flat feet — also called fallen arches or pes planus — is a common condition in which the foot arch collapses, causing the entire sole to touch the ground. Many people are asymptomatic, but others develop pain that can travel up the kinetic chain: ankle → knee → hip → lower back.

Key point: Flat feet affect more than the foot. When the arch collapses, forces through the lower limb change, often leading to knee valgus, hip shift, and lower-back stress.

How Flat Feet Affect the Knee

When the arch drops, the foot rolls inward excessively — called overpronation. That rotation travels up the tibia and affects knee alignment. The knee often drifts inward, increasing medial compartment stress.

Pes planus: the medial arch contacts the ground during standing.
  • Medial knee pain: Stress on the inner knee joint
  • Patellofemoral pain: Pain around or behind the kneecap
  • Cartilage irritation: Increased shear forces within the knee
  • Hip and back pain: Compensatory patterns above the knee

Foot Muscles: Intrinsic vs Extrinsic

Intrinsic Foot Muscles

These are small muscles inside the foot that support the arch from within. They provide fine stability and balance during gait and single-leg stance.

Extrinsic Foot Muscles

These are large lower-leg muscles connected to the foot via tendons. They generate the main propulsive and decelerating forces.

  • Tibialis Anterior: raises the medial arch
  • Peroneal tendons: stabilize the outer foot
  • Gastrocnemius/Soleus: provide push-off force
  • Flexor Hallucis Longus / Tibialis Posterior: deep dynamic arch support

Treatment: Intrinsic Muscle Exercises

These exercises target small stabilizers in the foot. Use a smooth, hard surface for towel drills so the towel slides easily.

1. Towel Scrunches

Goal: intrinsic foot muscles + arch support

  1. Sit on a chair with a smooth towel on a smooth floor
  2. Place your foot on the towel
  3. Scunch the towel toward you using your toes
  4. Release and repeat 10–15 times per foot

2. Marble Pick-Up / Hacky Sack

Picking up small objects with the toes improves arch control and toe mobility.

Goal: toe coordination + intrinsic activation

  1. Spread marbles or a small Hacky Sack on the floor
  2. Pick them up with your toes only
  3. Place them in a container
  4. Hacky Sack is more controlled and fun than towel drills

3. Toe Separators

Toe separators help restore natural toe splay so foot muscles can function correctly.

Goal: metatarsal spread, toe mobility, arch activation

  1. Use Correct Toes or similar separators
  2. Start with 15–30 minute sessions daily
  3. Progress to wearing them during exercise and walking
  4. Goal: comfortable 1-hour wear, even during jogging

Treatment: Extrinsic Muscle Exercises

These larger muscles are essential for absorbing landing forces and protecting the knee.

1. Tibialis Raise

Goal: Tibialis Anterior + dynamic arch lift

  1. Stand with feet hip-width apart
  2. Keep toes and outer foot flat on the ground
  3. Raise the medial arch as high as possible
  4. Hold 2 seconds, lower slowly
  5. 1–2 sets daily, 15–20 reps per foot

2. Calf Raises

Goal: Gastrocnemius and Soleus strength for shock absorption

  1. Beginner: both feet, holding wall support
  2. Intermediate: single leg, 20 reps
  3. Advanced: further from wall, single leg, loaded

Orthotics, Shoes and Common Mistakes

Supportive shoes matter — but relying on orthotics alone can weaken foot muscles over time.
  • Orthotics: short-term support and pain relief
  • Supportive shoes: prevent further arch collapse
  • Common mistake: orthotics only. Over time this can make the foot weaker
  • Solution: use orthotics as a bridge while strengthening intrinsic muscles

Lifestyle Modifications

  1. Maintain a healthy weight
  2. Stretch calves and hamstrings regularly
  3. Use ice after painful activity
  4. Progress gradually — avoid jumping to high volume quickly

When to See a Doctor

  • Severe or persistent knee pain
  • Sudden adult-onset flat feet
  • Pain limiting daily activity or sport
  • Doctor may advise imaging, MRI, or custom orthotics

Suggested Daily Routine

  1. Morning: 2 sets tibialis raises per leg
  2. Evening: 2 sets calf raises
  3. Any time: Hacky Sack / marble pickups while watching TV
  4. Throughout day: short sessions with toe separators
