Brave Browser: Privacy-First Open Source Browsing

← Back to Open Source

Overview

Brave is a free and open-source web browser built on Chromium that puts user privacy at the centre of the browsing experience. Founded by Brendan Eich, the creator of JavaScript and co-founder of Mozilla, Brave launched in 2016 with a clear mission: fix a broken web economy where users are tracked, ads are invasive, and pages load slowly under the weight of third-party scripts. Today, Brave has over 60 million monthly active users and is available on Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, and iOS.

What makes Brave different from other privacy browsers is its default behaviour: it blocks ads, trackers, fingerprinting attempts, and malicious scripts out of the box — no extensions, no configuration required. This results in faster page loads, significantly reduced data usage, and a safer browsing environment. Brave also pioneered a unique rewards system that lets users earn Basic Attention Tokens (BAT) by viewing privacy-respecting ads, and then tip or contribute those tokens to the content creators they support.

Key Features

  • Built-in Ad & Tracker Blocking: Brave Shields automatically blocks third-party ads, trackers, cross-site cookies, and fingerprinting attempts. Unlike browser extensions, Shields works at the browser level for better performance and a degree of protection that can't be bypassed by sites detecting common ad-block extensions.
  • Brave Shields Panel: A shield icon in the address bar shows a real-time breakdown of what has been blocked on the current site — ads, trackers, scripts, and more. You can toggle protections per-site and even per-request, giving you fine-grained control over your security posture.
  • Brave Rewards & BAT: An opt-in system where you earn Basic Attention Tokens (BAT) for viewing privacy-respecting ads. You can then automatically or manually tip your favourite websites, content creators, and YouTubers — all while keeping your browsing history private.
  • Brave Wallet & Web3: A built-in cryptocurrency wallet requiring no extension, supporting Ethereum, Solana, and other Web3 ecosystems. Brave also includes an IPFS node for decentralised file sharing and a built-in encrypted VPN service (paid).
  • Chromium Compatibility: Because Brave is built on Chromium, it supports the full Chrome Web Store library of extensions and delivers excellent compatibility with modern websites and web applications.
  • Private Browsing with Tor: Brave's private windows can be launched with Tor integration, routing your traffic through the Tor network for maximum anonymity when you need it.

Why Choose Brave?

Speed is one of Brave's most tangible benefits. By blocking trackers and ads at the network level, pages typically load 2–6 times faster than with conventional browsers, and data usage drops by up to 60% on mobile. Battery life improves too, because fewer scripts are executing in the background. For everyday users, this simply means a snappier, cheaper, and less distracting web experience.

Privacy is the other pillar. Brave's default settings already give you a level of protection that would require several separate extensions to achieve elsewhere, and its anti-fingerprinting measures make it substantially harder for advertisers to build a profile of you across the web. Crucially, all of this happens without selling your data: Brave's business model is based on its opt-in ad system, not on surveillance. Combined with the ability to sync encrypted bookmarks and settings across devices without an account, Brave delivers serious privacy for people who don't want to become privacy power users.
